Flooring is never used edge to edge with nothing left over. Boards get cut to fit the walls. Tiles get trimmed at the doorway. Some pieces are marked, chipped or too short to use. The extra you add to cover this is called the waste factor.

Every figure here is an estimate. Check the coverage printed on your own box, and ask your supplier about the waste they suggest for your layout.

## The short version

Use this formula:

**Area to buy = floor area × (1 + waste ÷ 100).**

Then divide by the area one box covers and round up to whole boxes. You cannot buy half a box.

## Why you need waste at all

Four things use up flooring that ends up on the floor as nothing:

- **Cuts at the walls.** The last board in each row is rarely the right width. The cut-off piece is often too short to start the next row.
- **Doorways, corners and alcoves.** Every awkward shape means more cutting.
- **Damaged pieces.** A few boards arrive with a chipped edge or a poor finish.
- **Mistakes.** A wrong measurement or a bad cut is easier to fix when you have a spare piece.

A bigger, plainer room wastes less. A small room with many corners wastes more.

## Step 1: Measure the floor area

Measure the length and the width, wall to wall. Multiply them together. A room 4 m by 3.5 m has a floor area of 14 m².

For a room that is not a rectangle, split it into rectangles. Measure each one and add the areas together. The guide on [how to measure a room](/guides/how-to-measure-a-room/) shows how.

## Step 2: Pick the waste for your layout

The way you lay the floor changes how much you cut. Roomtally starts from these typical allowances:

| Layout | Typical waste |
| --- | --- |
| Straight (boards or tiles parallel to the walls) | 10% |
| Diagonal | 15% |
| Herringbone | 20% |

These are typical trade allowances, not rules. Complicated rooms, patterned planks or a very small floor may need more. A large, plain room may need a little less.

You can change the waste in the flooring options. The Assumptions panel always shows the value in use.

## Step 3: Add the waste

Multiply the floor area by one plus the waste as a decimal. For 10%, that is 1.10. For 15%, it is 1.15. For 20%, it is 1.20.

## Step 4: Round up to whole boxes

Flooring is sold in boxes. Each box covers a set area, printed on the label. Divide the area with waste by the box coverage, then round up.

Always round up. Buying one box too few means a second order, and a second order can come from a different batch.

## Worked example: a 4 m by 3.5 m room

The floor is 4 × 3.5 = 14 m². The boxes cover 2 m² each.

| Layout | Working | Area with waste | Boxes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight, 10% | 14 × 1.10 | 15.4 m² | 8 |
| Diagonal, 15% | 14 × 1.15 | 16.1 m² | 9 |
| Herringbone, 20% | 14 × 1.20 | 16.8 m² | 9 |

For the straight lay, 15.4 ÷ 2 is 7.7, so you buy 8 boxes. They cover 16 m².

For the diagonal lay, 16.1 ÷ 2 is 8.05. That rounds up to 9 boxes, not 8, because 8 boxes cover only 16 m². The list shows the buy quantity first and the unrounded need beside it.

## Spare boxes for repairs

Waste covers what you use up while fitting. A spare box is different. It sits in a cupboard in case a board is damaged years later.

Boards from the same batch match best. A box bought later may differ slightly in shade. If you want a repair reserve, add a box to your order, or enter a higher waste figure.

## Fitting more than one room

If several rooms get the same flooring, add their areas together first. Apply the waste once, then round up once.

Take the 14 m² room and a 6 m² hallway. Both use the same straight lay and 2 m² boxes.

- Together: 20 × 1.10 = 22 m², which fills 11 boxes.
- Rounded one at a time: 8 boxes for the room and 4 for the hall. That is 12 boxes.

The hall needs 6.6 m², which is 3.3 boxes, so on its own it rounds up to 4.

Adding the rooms first saves a box here. Roomtally does this for you. It adds the rooms together first and rounds up once.

## Does the waste factor change for tiles?

The idea is the same. Tiles are cut at the edges, and a diagonal or herringbone layout wastes more. Roomtally uses the same typical allowances for tile. The tile calculator also adds a grout gap to each tile, which changes how many tiles fit.

## Common mistakes

- Using the floor area as the amount to buy, with no waste.
- Forgetting to round up to whole boxes.
- Using the waste for a straight lay when the pattern is diagonal.
- Measuring a room in feet and entering the number as metres.
- Buying boxes from different batches, so the shade varies across the floor.
