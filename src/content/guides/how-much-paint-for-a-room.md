Paint is easy to under-buy and easy to over-buy. A few minutes of measuring fixes both. This guide shows how to work out the litres for one room, then turn that into real cans.

Every figure here is an estimate. Check the coverage printed on your own tin before you buy.

## The short version

Use this formula:

**Litres = wall area × coats ÷ coverage, plus a little spare.**

Then round up to the can sizes the shop sells. The steps below explain each part.

## Step 1: Measure the room

Measure the length and the width along the floor, wall to wall. Measure the height from the floor to the ceiling. Write the numbers down.

Use one unit for everything. If you measure in feet, enter feet. If you measure in metres, enter metres. Mixing the two is the most common reason for a result that looks wrong.

## Step 2: Find the wall area

Add the length and the width. Double the result. Multiply by the height. That is the area of all four walls.

Take a room 4 m by 3.5 m with a 2.4 m ceiling. The working is 2 × (4 + 3.5) × 2.4, which gives 36 m².

## Step 3: Take off doors and windows

Measure each door and window. Multiply the width by the height. Add the areas together and subtract the total from the wall area.

Small items, such as light switches and sockets, can be ignored. They are too small to change the number of cans.

## Step 4: Choose the number of coats

Two coats is the usual advice when you change colour. One coat can be enough to refresh the same colour on walls in good shape.

Bare plaster, a stained wall or a big colour change may need more. A coat of primer first can reduce the number of finish coats. Roomtally lets you choose from one to four coats.

## Step 5: Divide by the coverage

Coverage tells you how much area one litre covers in one coat. It is printed on the tin. A common figure is 10 square metres per litre. In the US, a common figure is about 350 square feet per gallon.

Rough or porous walls soak up more paint than smooth ones. If your wall is rough, your real coverage may be lower than the label says.

## Step 6: Add a little spare

A small spare amount covers touch-ups and uneven walls. Roomtally adds 5% by default. You can change it in the paint options, and the Assumptions panel shows the value in use.

## Step 7: Round up to real cans

Paint is sold in set sizes, such as 1, 2.5, 5 and 10 litres. You cannot buy 6.88 litres. You buy cans that add up to at least that much.

Always round up. Running short half way through a wall can leave a visible line when you open a second batch.

## Worked example: a 4 m by 3.5 m bedroom

The ceiling is 2.4 m high. The room has one door (0.9 m by 2 m) and one window (1.2 m by 1.2 m).

| Step | Working | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All four walls | 2 × (4 + 3.5) × 2.4 | 36 m² |
| Door and window | 0.9 × 2 + 1.2 × 1.2 | 3.24 m² |
| Wall area to paint | 36 − 3.24 | 32.76 m² |
| Two coats at 10 m² per litre | 32.76 × 2 ÷ 10 | 6.552 L |
| Plus 5% spare | 6.552 × 1.05 | 6.88 L |

You need about 6.88 litres. With cans of 1, 2.5, 5 and 10 litres, the closest fit is 7 litres. That is one 5 litre can and two 1 litre cans, which is three cans.

The list shows the buy quantity first and the unrounded need beside it. Here that is 3 cans to buy and 6.88 litres needed.

## Ceiling and primer

The ceiling is a separate line, because it is often a different paint. In the same bedroom the ceiling is 4 × 3.5 = 14 m². Two coats with spare comes to 14 × 2 ÷ 10 × 1.05 = 2.94 litres.

Primer goes on in one coat. For the walls of the example room that is 32.76 ÷ 10 × 1.05, or about 3.44 litres.

Switch each one on in the paint options when you want it in your list.

## Painting more than one room

If several rooms get the same paint, add their litres together before you round up. Rounding each room on its own leaves a part-used can in every room.

Roomtally adds the rooms first and rounds once. The whole-project estimator does this for up to 10 rooms.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the second coat.
- Entering feet in a box that is set to metres, or the other way round.
- Leaving out the ceiling, then buying again later.
- Buying cans from different batches. Colour can vary slightly between batches, so buy everything you need together.
