Every estimate starts with a few measurements. Get them right once and the same numbers work for paint, flooring, tile and wallpaper. Beautally is built around this idea: enter a room once, then read one shopping list.

All results are estimates. Measure carefully, and check your supplier's figures before you buy.

## What you need

- A tape measure, at least 5 metres or 16 feet long.
- A pencil and paper, or your phone.
- A second person for long walls, if you can.

## The four numbers every room needs

You need the length, the width and the ceiling height. Doors and windows come after.

### Length and width

Measure along the floor, from wall to wall. Skirting boards, built-in cupboards and chimney breasts can make the floor look smaller than the walls. For flooring, measure the space you will cover. For paint, measure the full wall.

Measure twice. Old houses often have walls that are not quite square, so the two lengths on opposite sides can differ. If they do, use the larger number. A small overestimate is safer than running short.

### Ceiling height

Measure from the floor to the ceiling in the middle of the room. Standard rooms are around 2.4 m, or 8 feet, but older and newer homes vary. A slope or a sloping attic ceiling needs more care, so measure the highest and lowest points.

Beautally accepts heights from 1.5 m to 6 m.

### Doors and windows

Measure each door and window by width and height. Include the frame if you will not paint or paper it. Beautally takes the area off the walls.

Skip tiny items such as light switches and sockets. They do not change how much you buy.

You can add up to 20 openings to a room. Give each one a width, a height and a count. Three identical windows are one entry with a count of 3.

## Which numbers does each material use?

| Material  | Uses                                                                        |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Paint     | Wall area (length, width, height, minus openings). The ceiling is optional. |
| Flooring  | Floor area (length × width).                                                |
| Tile      | Floor area, or wall area for wall tiles.                                    |
| Wallpaper | Wall area, plus the ceiling height for each strip.                          |

That is why one set of measurements is enough.

## Keep one unit

Choose metric or imperial before you start. Write every number down in that unit. The most common cause of a strange result is feet typed into a form set to metres.

Beautally stores your sizes in metres and converts what it shows. Switching units does not change the maths.

## Rooms that are not rectangles

Beautally works with rectangles. Most rooms can be split into rectangles, and each rectangle goes in as its own room. Rooms are added together for the final list.

For an L-shaped room:

1. Draw the room and split it into two rectangles.
2. Measure each rectangle.
3. Enter both as rooms.
4. Add the join between them as an opening in each room, so the shared line is not counted as wall.

The join is only needed for paint and wallpaper. Floors and tiles just add up.

## Worked example: an L-shaped room

An L-shaped room splits into part A, 5 m by 3 m, and part B, 2 m by 2 m. The ceiling is 2.4 m high. The join between them is 2 m wide.

| Step                        | Working           | Result  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------- | ------- |
| Floor, part A               | 5 × 3             | 15 m²   |
| Floor, part B               | 2 × 2             | 4 m²    |
| Total floor                 | 15 + 4            | 19 m²   |
| Walls, part A               | 2 × (5 + 3) × 2.4 | 38.4 m² |
| Walls, part B               | 2 × (2 + 2) × 2.4 | 19.2 m² |
| Join, counted in both parts | 2 × 2.4 × 2       | 9.6 m²  |
| Wall area to paint          | 38.4 + 19.2 − 9.6 | 48 m²   |

Without the join, the wall total would read 57.6 m². That is nearly a fifth too high, and you would buy extra paint.

In Beautally, add the 2 m by 2.4 m join as an opening in part A. Add it again in part B.

## Check your numbers

Before you trust a list, check three things:

- Does the floor area look right? A bedroom of 14 m² is a typical size.
- Are the wall heights the same as the ceiling height you measured?
- Did you include every door and window?

If the number looks odd, measure again. Beautally shows an error code next to any value outside its limits.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring to the skirting board for paint, and so missing a few centimetres of wall.
- Forgetting a door, then buying too much paint.
- Entering a measurement in the wrong unit.
- Measuring only one side in an uneven room.
- Counting a shared line in an L-shaped room twice.
